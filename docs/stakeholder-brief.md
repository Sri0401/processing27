# Campus Operations: what the new data platform tells us

*For: Head of Estates, Facilities Managers, Sustainability and Finance leads · One page*

We now bring **hourly meter data from 147 buildings** together with **maintenance, asset condition and accessibility records** in one place, refreshed automatically, with dashboards anyone can open. Here is what it shows, and what we suggest doing.

---

### 1. We are paying for energy in empty buildings: about £222,000 a year is avoidable

- In 2016 the campus spent **£3.87M on energy** and emitted **18,500 tonnes of CO₂e**.
- Teaching buildings use **43% of their electricity when they are closed** (evenings, nights, weekends). Some use much more than similar buildings: *Terina* uses 54% out of hours where comparable teaching buildings use 42%.
- If every building ran out of hours like a typical building of its type, we would save **£222k and 1,100 tCO₂e a year**, about 6% of the energy bill.
- **The ten largest opportunities make up 42% of the saving (£94k).**

**Suggested action:** review heating, ventilation and lighting time schedules for those ten opportunities with the BMS contractor. This is low cost, needs no capital, and is measurable within a month on the *Campus Energy* dashboard.

### 2. Most maintenance money is spent on breakdowns, not prevention

- In 2017, **69% of maintenance spend was reactive** (fixing failures). Good practice is around 30% reactive and 70% planned.
- Emergency (P1) jobs met their response target only **78% of the time**.
- The **maintenance backlog is about £34.5M**. **240 critical assets** (boilers, electrical distribution, lifts, fire alarms) are in poor or life-expired condition and are rated **replace now**.
- The biggest backlog items are **windows (£7.4M), air-handling units (£5.7M) and boilers (£4.0M)**.

**Suggested action:** use the *Investment priority list* to sequence the capital programme. Fund planned maintenance for the assets with the most repeat breakdowns first; each avoided emergency call-out is cheaper than the repair it prevents.

### 3. Accessibility: 53 buildings need attention

- **53 buildings are rated Red** (a high-severity failure or under 60% compliant), 42 Amber and 52 Green.
- The most common serious gaps are **step-free entrances (25 buildings), accessible toilets (21) and visual fire alarms (21)**.
- **Lift access** is the most expensive gap (about **£2.1M** across 20 buildings). Visual fire-alarm beacons are cheap (about £73k in total) and safety-critical.
- Pre-1945 buildings average 78% compliance; buildings since 2004 average 90%.

**Suggested action:** fund the low-cost, high-severity fixes (beacons, hearing loops) now, and add lift and entrance works to the capital plan alongside the asset backlog in the same buildings.

---

### How far can you trust these numbers?

- **Energy figures** come from real meter readings. About 18% of readings were faulty (meters stuck or offline) and are **excluded**, not guessed. The *Platform Health* dashboard shows which meters need fixing.
- **Costs** use assumed contract tariffs, and **carbon** uses UK Government conversion factors. Finance can update both without any code changes.
- **In this demonstration, the maintenance, condition and accessibility records are simulated** to realistic patterns. With our real CAFM and survey exports, the same dashboards fill in with actual figures.
